1. What you are getting

A browser extension that records a testing session on your own machine and turns it into evidence: a timeline, the cause of each failure, and the test documents the ISTQB and ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3 shapes name.

There is no QA Wrap account, no QA Wrap server and no subscription. Nothing here is a hosted service, so there is nothing for Nader Alzghoul to take away, and nothing that stops working if a server goes down. SLA.md, carried in the package, explains which of those guarantees hold because of how the software is built and which depend on Chrome.

Two other documents are part of the same agreement, and neither is superseded by this one: LICENSE sets out what you may and may not do with the software itself, and the privacy policy sets out what the extension records and where it goes. Where this page and either of those appear to disagree, the more specific one governs.

2. The licensing model

QA Wrap is free and open source under the MIT licence. There is no seat count, no account and no payment.

You may use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense and sell copies, provided the copyright and permission notice stay with them. 3. Your testing, and what comes out of it

The reports, defect records, screenshots, recordings and test cases you produce from your own testing are yours. The one obligation attached to output is attribution of the tool: generated artefacts carry an attribution notice and a provenance token, and removing or falsifying that mark, or presenting QA Wrap's output as another tool's, is bad faith toward the people who read your report. This is about crediting the tool, not about owning the findings.

QA Wrap keeps what it records on your own machine. It has no server and no account, so Nader Alzghoul holds no copy of your sessions and cannot retrieve them for you. The privacy policy is the detail, and it is part of these terms.

4. What you are responsible for

You choose what to test and what to record. That makes a few things yours to get right, and the extension gives you the controls for each:

  1. Permission to test the site. QA Wrap works on whatever site you point it at, including sites you do not own. Having the technical ability to record a site is not authorisation to test it.
  2. The personal data you capture. Testing happens on systems full of real people's records. Masking, redaction and a retention window are in Settings, documented in the privacy policy, and it is your decision whether to use them. Under GDPR you are the controller for anything you record; Nader Alzghoul is not a processor of it, because none of it reaches Nader Alzghoul.
  3. Where you send it. Jira, Slack, Teams, Discord, Google Chat, GitHub, Linear, Trello, ClickUp and Asana are opt-in, configured by you, and nothing is sent until you press a button naming the destination. Once a report leaves for one of them, that service's terms apply to it and this one no longer can.
  4. The tokens you paste. A tracker token stored in your browser profile is readable by anyone with access to that profile. Scope it to what it needs and revoke it when you are done.

5. What the extension will not do

These are commitments, and the privacy policy documents the mechanism behind each:

  • It makes no network request of its own. No analytics, no telemetry, no update ping, no remote code.
  • It does not record password fields or cookie values into a session or an export. Saved cookie profiles are the exception: they hold the values you saved, on your machine, until you delete them.
  • It records nothing and acts on nothing in a browser window you have not woken, including through the right-click menu and the keyboard shortcuts. On a site you have allowed, its page scripts stay loaded.
